## 1. Who they actually are

**Legal name:** חברת קיסר שיווק וניהול נכסים בע"מ. Trading as **קיסר ניהול נכסים**;
the parent brand is **קבוצת קיסר**.
**HQ:** החשמונאים 96, תל אביב. Branches described only as "בפריסה ארצית"; the one
branch with a page of its own is **סניף חיפה, נשר, קריות והצפון**.
**Contact:** 1599-55-66-55 (national), 052-5416313 (direct), info@caesar.co.il,
facebook.com/caesar.co.il (1,979 followers).

They describe themselves as a brand "אשר נבנה במשך מעל עשר שנים" — elsewhere on the
same site, "11 שנה". Note the site is stale: those two numbers were written years
apart and neither has been updated. **Do not print an age figure in the rebuild
unless the client confirms one.** The safe, sourced figures are the property count
and the survey score.

The group runs three divisions, per their own numbered list plus the nav:

1. **המחלקה לניהול נכסים** — the commercial centre of gravity, and roughly 90% of
   the site's surface area.
2. **בניה־ניהול־יזמות** — תמ"א 38, פינוי בינוי, קבוצות רכישה, שיפוצים, פיקוח בניה.
3. **יזמות עסקים והשקעות** — קרן קיסר, מועדון משקיעים, business mentoring.

A fourth thing exists in practice but not in the nav: **קיסר מסחריים**, a commercial
brokerage arm visible only through the Facebook embed on the homepage, currently
pushing combination deals, data-centre plots, hotels, logistics space and care homes.

> "ההתמחות של קיסר היא להוריד את כל כאבי הראש מבעל הנכס ולבצע את כל הניהול של הנכס

## 2. Who they sell to

Four buyers, in descending order of how much site real estate each gets:

1. **The absent flat owner.** Owns one to five units, does not want to be a landlord.
   Often abroad (רילוקיישן, תושבי ומשקיעי חוץ), often inheriting (דירות עיזבון), often
   elderly (גיל הזהב). Their fear is not low yield — it is *phone calls*. The product
   sold to them is silence. Named testimonial evidence: "מאז שהחלו לטפל בנכסים שלנו –
   הוסרו מאיתנו כל הדאגות."
2. **The income-property owner and mall owner.** Wants tenant mix, occupancy and cost
   control. Their language is תמהיל, תשואה, השבחה, הקטנת הוצאות. They benchmark against
   managers who charge "עד לכ-15% דמי ניהול קניון".
3. **The developer/contractor** buying project marketing. Sold on method — "5 סוגי
   אסטרטגיות שיווק" and audience fit ("הדירות חייבות להתאים לקהל היעד").
4. **The investor** buying a managed portfolio, offered a personal advisor at no cost.

There is a fifth audience the site keeps addressing — **prospective franchisees and
brokers** ("דרושים זכיינים ושותפים להצלחה"). It sits awkwardly beside the owner-facing
pitch and I would push it off the home page to its own route in the rebuild.

## 3. Tone of voice

**What theirs is now:** warm, direct, second-person, slightly breathless. Heavy
exclamation marks and ellipses. Superlatives stacked without evidence ("מושלם" appears
in almost every paragraph). Rhetorical questions as headings ("למה כדאי לקחת חברה
לניהול נכסים? ולמה דווקא אנחנו?"). Long keyword-stuffed titles built for 2015 SEO
("ניהול נכסים בבת ים | ניהול נכסים ללא תשלום | אחזקת מבנים בבת ים | …"). Occasional
slang — "אחלה שירות", "מנהל נכסים חאפר". Typos and inconsistent numbers throughout.

**What to keep:** the second person, the plain speech, the willingness to name the
customer's actual problem (headaches, worries, a bad previous manager), the slightly
combative honesty of "עשרת הסימנים שאתם חייבים להחליף את המנהל נכסים שלכם".

**What to drop:** the exclamation marks, the pipe-stuffed headings, and above all the
word **מושלם**. It appears so often it has stopped meaning anything. Replace the claim
of perfection with the evidence they already have — 300 properties, 9.6, six years with
one client, a named professor in Haifa.

**The voice for the rebuild, in one line:** *quietly competent, specific, and slightly
protective of the owner.* Short declaratives. Numbers instead of adjectives. Never
"מושלם"; if a sentence needs it, the sentence is not carrying a fact.

## 4. Colour

### Extracted — with an honest caveat

I could not sample pixels. This environment blocks all direct HTTP, so I never
retrieved the stylesheet or a single image file; only text came back through search.
What I can say from evidence is structural, not chromatic: the site runs the
**OpenDoor 1.4** WordPress theme (visible in an asset path,
`/wp-content/themes/OpenDoor1.4/images/downarrow.png`), a mid-2010s real-estate theme
whose stock palette is a saturated blue-grey chrome over white cards with an orange
call-to-action accent. Their imagery divides cleanly into two families: warm-lit
commercial interiors (shop windows, mall concourses) and cool exterior tower glass.

Treat the table below as **the theme's likely palette, unverified**, not as sampled
brand colour. Ask the client for the logo file before anything ships.

| Role | Likely current | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chrome / header | `#2A3B4D` | OpenDoor default dark slate |
| Accent / CTA | `#E8762C` | theme's stock orange |
| Body text | `#4A4A4A` | |
| Page ground | `#FFFFFF` / `#F5F5F5` | |

### Proposed

The name is the brief. **קיסר / Caesar** — an imperial name attached to a company whose
actual promise is *custody*: your asset is looked after while you are not looking. So:
a deep, slightly green-black as the ground (stone, night, the building at rest), a
restrained brass as the single accent (the imperial note, and literally the metal of
keys, plaques and door furniture), and a cool daylight neutral for surfaces. No orange,
no "trust blue" — every property manager in Israel uses one or the other.

| Token | Hex | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `--caesar-ink` | `#12181C` | Page ground on dark sections; primary text on light |
| `--caesar-stone` | `#1E2A30` | Elevated dark surfaces, header, footer |
| `--caesar-brass` | `#B8873F` | The accent. Rules, active states, stat figures, key line in a heading |
| `--caesar-brass-lift` | `#D6A85C` | Hover / focus only — brass on dark needs the lift to clear 4.5:1 |
| `--caesar-limestone` | `#F4F1EC` | Light-section ground. Warm, not clinical white |
| `--caesar-slate` | `#5A6A72` | Secondary text, captions, meta |
| `--caesar-line` | `#D8D2C8` | Hairlines on limestone |
| `--caesar-line-dark` | `#2C3A41` | Hairlines on ink |
| `--caesar-signal` | `#3F7D6E` | Positive/verified only — the 9.6, "0% עמלות". A muted green so it reads as a fact, not a sale |

Contrast: `--caesar-limestone` on `--caesar-ink` is ~15:1. `--caesar-brass` on
`--caesar-ink` is ~5.4:1 — fine for text 16px+ and for large figures, and it is why
`--caesar-brass-lift` exists for small interactive text. `--caesar-slate` on
`--caesar-limestone` is ~4.7:1. Verify all of these against the real tokens in the
build; do not take my arithmetic on faith.

Ratio discipline: brass is an accent, not a brand colour. If more than ~5% of any
viewport is brass, it has stopped reading as gold and started reading as a
2011 real-estate site.

## 5. Typography

Hebrew first. The face has to carry long compound headings
("ניהול ואחזקת נכסים בפריסה ארצית") at display size without the counters closing up,
and dense body Hebrew at 17–18px without shimmering.

**Display / headings — Heebo.**
```css
--font-display: "Heebo", "Assistant", "Rubik",
                "Noto Sans Hebrew", "Arial Hebrew", "Segoe UI",
                system-ui, sans-serif;
```
Heebo is Hebrew-first (Roboto's Latin married to Oved Ben-Yehuda's Hebrew), ships a
real 200–900 range, and holds its shape at 64px+. Ship weights 400 and 700 only.

**Body — Assistant.**
```css
--font-body: "Assistant", "Heebo", "Noto Sans Hebrew",
             "Arial Hebrew", "Segoe UI", system-ui, sans-serif;
```
Assistant has a taller Hebrew x-height and slightly looser default spacing than
Heebo, which is what long RTL paragraphs need. Weights 400 and 600.

**Numerals / stat figures — Heebo tabular.** Set `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums`
on the stats block so 300 / 9.6 / 92 / 99 align on the baseline grid. Latin-script
figures inside RTL text need `direction: ltr; unicode-bidi: isolate` or "9.6" will
render as "6.9" beside a Hebrew word.

**Rules that matter in RTL**
- Never letter-space Hebrew. `letter-spacing` on Hebrew is a bug, not a style.
- Line height 1.65 minimum for Hebrew body — Hebrew has no ascenders/descenders to
  create optical spacing, so it needs more leading than Latin at the same size.
- Split headings **by line only**, never by character. Per `contracts/motion.spec.md`,
  and because per-character splitting destroys glyph shaping in several Hebrew faces.
- Set `text-wrap: balance` on display headings; RTL orphans read worse than LTR ones.
- Load only the Hebrew + Latin subsets. Two families × two weights × two subsets is the
  whole budget.

## 6. Visual metaphors

Five, each specific to what this company actually does. Not skylines, not handshakes,
not a hand holding a small model house.

1. **The key that never leaves the desk.**
   The owner hands over one key and stops thinking about the building. Visually: a
   single key or fob on a dark desk, shallow depth of field, the building itself only
   as a soft reflection in the surface behind it. This is the "כאבי ראש" promise made
   literal — custody, not ownership transfer. Best as the hero.

2. **Lit windows at dusk, counted.**
   A managed building at the hour when occupancy becomes visible. Every lit window is
   a paying tenant; every dark one is the thing Caesar exists to fix. Pairs directly
   with the 300 figure and with the regional counts, and gives the stats section a
   background that is doing argumentative work rather than decoration.

3. **The service corridor.**
   Riser, meter bank, plant room, the back-of-house of a mall — the parts of a
   property that only a manager ever sees. Nobody in this category photographs these,
   which is exactly why they are worth photographing. Says "אחזקת מבנים" without a
   single word, and gives the commercial/maintenance sections a look nobody else has.

4. **Tenant mix as a lit floorplate.**
   For malls and commercial centres: an overhead or long-exposure view of a concourse
   where footfall traces the plan. Their own word for the job is **תמהיל** — the mix.
   This is the mix, made visible. Natural home for the horizontal-scroll sequence.

5. **Before and after, in the same frame.**
   השבחה is a core service and the only one with an inherently visual proof. A single
   interior held across a renovation, with a hard vertical seam — the seam moving on
   scroll in the showcase, static under `prefers-reduced-motion`.

**Explicitly out of bounds:** generic skylines, sunset-over-Tel-Aviv drone shots,
stock handshakes, keys being passed between two hands, a family standing outside a
new house, anything with a "SOLD" sign. Their current image library is full of these
and it is the single biggest reason the site reads as 2015.
